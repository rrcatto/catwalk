Earth science

Where does rain come from?

The same water has been going round for billions of years.

Every drop of rain has fallen before. In the next few minutes we follow one drop all the way round.

Water is never used up. It only moves and changes form.

The idea at the heart of the water cycle

Keep this one idea in mind: nothing is lost, it only moves.

The cycle has four stages

  1. Evaporation: the sun warms water into vapour
  2. Condensation: the vapour cools into cloud droplets
  3. Precipitation: the droplets fall as rain, hail or snow
  4. Collection: the water gathers in seas, lakes and the ground
Press Next to reveal each stage in turn.

Vapour and cloud are not the same

Water vapour

  • A gas
  • Invisible
  • Always in the air around us

A cloud

Tiny drops of liquid water or ice crystals, floating because they are so small. That is why you can see it.

One drop's journey

  1. Morning Sun warms the sea and the drop evaporates
  2. Afternoon It rises, cools and joins a cloud
  3. Evening It falls as rain on a hillside
  4. Weeks later A river carries it back to the sea

Make a cloud in a jar

  1. Pour a little hot water into a glass jar
  2. Put a plate of ice on top of the jar
  3. Watch the warm, damp air cool under the ice
  4. See droplets form a small cloud inside

The cold glass

On a hot day you take a glass of iced water outside. Within minutes the outside of the glass is wet, though none spilled.

Where did the water on the glass come from?

From the air. The cold glass cooled the vapour around it until it condensed, the same step that makes clouds.

Which stage turns vapour into cloud droplets?

  1. Evaporation
  2. Condensation
  3. Precipitation
Show the answer

Condensation. As vapour rises it cools, and cooler air holds less vapour, so it condenses into droplets.
